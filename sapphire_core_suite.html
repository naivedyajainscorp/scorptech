Workplace Management

Workplace Management is the foundation layer of Sapphire. Before you can track assets, assign work orders or manage inventory — your locations need to exist as structured, intelligent profiles. This module is where you build the operational skeleton of your entire organisation — every branch, outlet, facility or site — and then operate all of them from a single unified interface in real time.

It is not a directory. It is a live command centre.

Key Features

Unlimited Workplaces Under One Organisation

No cap on how many locations you register. A single-outlet business and a 50-branch enterprise operate on the same platform without any architectural difference.

Overlapping Shift Support

Define shifts that intentionally overlap in time across different employee groups. A 24-hour facility with rotating teams, a hospital with morning and afternoon staff during handover, a call centre with staggered start times — all managed without workarounds.

Open / Closed Status Intelligence

Each workplace automatically reads as open or closed based on whether active shifts are currently running against the defined workplace hours. No manual toggle. No one forgetting to update a status board.

Real-Time Asset Snapshot Per Location

The moment you switch to a workplace, you see a complete, live count of every asset category — broken down by operational status. You know immediately if tools are missing, if equipment is under maintenance or if vehicles are assigned.

Live Activity Log

A continuously updating event stack shows every action occurring at the workplace in real time — assignments, check-ins, inspections, maintenance triggers, inventory movements. Not a report. Not a notification. A live operational feed.

Role-Based Access and Visibility

The owner has full control and visibility across all workplaces. The Prime Manager of a workplace can modify shift timings for their location. Workers and staff can view non-confidential workplace details — name, contact, address.

Inspection on Log-in

A mandatory toolbox inspection gate on first daily log-in per workplace. Configurable per location. Eliminates the gap between "shift started" and "equipment verified."

Why Do I Need This?

If you operate more than one location, you already know the problem. Updates come when someone remembers to send them. You find out about an issue at Branch 3 on a Tuesday morning because the branch manager mentioned it during a call about something else entirely.

Sapphire does not wait for someone to report. It shows you everything, live, the moment you look.

The second problem is shift management. Most tools assume your team works one shift, five days a week. Real operations — especially in manufacturing, healthcare, hospitality and logistics — are staggered, overlapping and constantly adjusted. A system that cannot handle that forces you back to a spreadsheet sitting next to your "modern" software.

The third problem is asset accountability per location. You may have 3 branches and no idea how many tools are actually at each one right now, how many are assigned, how many are damaged or missing. Workplace Management solves this without a manual count or a phone call.

What Is User Management?

User Management is Sapphire's people layer — the module where every person who touches your operation gets a structured identity, a defined role and a precisely configured set of permissions. It connects your organisational hierarchy directly to operational access, ensuring that what someone can do in Sapphire is an exact reflection of what they are authorised to do on the ground.

It is not a contact book. It is an access control system built specifically for asset-intensive operations.

Key Features

Inventory-Level Access Control

Access is not just role-based — it is inventory-specific. The same employee can have full access to one inventory and read-only access to another. This is the operational precision most platforms cannot offer without expensive custom configuration.

23-Point Permission Matrix Per Employee

Every employee's permission set is configurable across 23 distinct action categories within their permitted inventories. No other configuration of the same employee affects another — each is independent, auditable and reversible.

Profile Change Log

Every modification made to an employee's profile — contact update, role change, permission adjustment, shift reassignment — is logged with a timestamp and the identity of who made the change. Nothing is silently edited.

Block / Unblock Without Deletion

Temporarily restrict an employee's access without removing their record, their history or their permissions. When the situation is resolved, access is restored in one action. No reconfiguration needed.

Document-Backed Termination

Employee exits are recorded formally — reason selected, supporting document attached and stored against the employee record. Compliance, HR and operational audit requirements are covered automatically.

Workstation-Aware Access

An employee's workstation location is not just display information — it defines which assets are visible in their immediate operational context when they log in. Someone at Ground Floor Division 2 sees the assets relevant to that location, not the entire facility.

Shared Employee Across Workplaces

Staff who work across multiple locations are supported natively. Their profile exists once and can be enrolled in multiple workplaces — they always see which branch of which organisation they are currently operating in.

Why Do I Need This?

The moment you have more than five people working in your operation, access starts to become a liability. Not because your people are untrustworthy — but because a system with no access structure relies entirely on everyone remembering what they should and should not do. That works until it does not.

The real cost of poor access control in an asset operation is not a security breach — it is asset accountability gaps. Items moved without a record. Inventory adjusted by someone who should not have had access. Financial details seen by employees who should not know what equipment cost. None of this shows up as a single incident — it accumulates as unexplained discrepancies, missing items and eroded trust in your own data.

Sapphire's permission matrix is designed specifically for the reality of operations — not for IT departments managing server access. The 23-point permission model reflects actual operational actions: issuing a tool, returning an item, sending equipment for service, viewing repair costs. Every permission maps to something real that happens in your facility every day.

The second reason is compliance. Industries with regulatory requirements — healthcare, pharmaceuticals, aviation, government facilities — require documented proof of who accessed what and when. Sapphire's audit trail and profile change log provide that automatically, without additional configuration.

What Is Master Data Management?

Master Data Management is the configuration layer of Sapphire — the module where you define the reference data that every other module draws from. Locations, brands, measurement units, tax categories, fuel types, job titles, departments, and holiday calendars — all of it is established here once, maintained in one place, and used consistently across the entire platform without duplication or inconsistency.

It is not administrative overhead. It is the structural foundation that makes every other operation in Sapphire accurate.

Key Features

Five-Level Location Hierarchy

Building → Floor → Room → Sub-division → Reference. Five levels of physical location classification that map the structure of any facility — from a single-floor workshop to a multi-building industrial campus — with enough precision to identify a specific shelf position as an asset's location.

Custom Measurement Units with Conversion Ratios

Define units that reflect how your operation actually measures consumption and issuance. Each custom unit is anchored to an SI base unit through a defined conversion ratio — ensuring accurate base-unit quantities for reporting and audit purposes.

Self-Managed Tax Configuration

Tax categories and rates are created, edited and removed entirely within Sapphire — independent of external APIs or platform release cycles. New tax regimes, revised rates and regional tax variations are handled immediately.

Dual Fuel Classification

Material-based and battery-grade fuel types are maintained as a reference list used across vehicle and equipment records. Fuel type definition is open — any fuel, any unit, any industry.

Centralised Brand Registry

A single maintained list of OEM brands that flows through every asset, part and material record in the platform — enabling consistent brand-level filtering, reporting and procurement visibility.

Recurring Holiday Rules

Annual and monthly repeat options on holiday entries mean the calendar maintains itself. Conflict resolution with weekly off days is automatic — no manual checking required when holidays shift days across years.

Master Data Audit Log

Every addition, edit and removal across all master data categories is recorded in a chronological audit log — timestamped and attributed. The log is maintained on a separate dedicated page.

Why Do I Need This?

The quality of every report, every asset record and every operational decision in Sapphire is directly determined by the quality of the master data it runs on. A location structure that tops out at two levels forces you to describe asset locations in free text — which becomes inconsistent the moment two different people type it differently. A measurement unit system that does not reflect your operational language forces your team to mentally convert every time they issue or consume something. A tax configuration that depends on an external API means a new purchase sits uncategorised until someone else updates something you cannot control.

Master Data Management eliminates all of these friction points at the source. You define your facility's physical language, your operational measurement language and your financial classification language — once, correctly, in one place. Every module in Sapphire inherits that definition and applies it consistently.

The audit log exists because master data changes are not trivial. A location renamed mid-operation, a measurement unit ratio corrected, a tax rate adjusted — each of these affects records already in the system. The log ensures that every change is traceable, every adjustment is documented and every discrepancy has an explanation.

What Is Inventory Management?

Inventory Management in Sapphire is built around a single structural principle: not all items in an operation exist in the same state, and a system that treats them identically fails all of them. A CNC machine sitting in storage waiting for seasonal deployment is not the same as a spanner assigned to a technician's toolbox, which is not the same as a cutting fluid being drawn from an active floor inventory by request. Each of these has a different physical reality, a different accountability requirement and a different operational workflow.

Sapphire formalises this through three inventory types — Reserve, Distributable Reserve and Active Inventory — each purpose-built for the category of item it manages.

The Three Inventory Types

Reserve

The storage layer — the back room, the tool store, the warehouse rack. Holds items not currently deployed but part of the operation's active asset pool. Reserve presents items in a grouped structure with per-unit drill-down — every individual unit listed with its current status: which inventory it is in, what condition it is in, whether it is assigned, under maintenance, missing or restricted.

Distributable Reserve

The Hand Tool Reserve — items assigned directly to individuals rather than circulated through request workflows. All units visible upfront without grouping. An authorised issuer can assign items directly to an employee's toolbox, view any employee's kit, and compare two employees' toolboxes side-by-side with transfers logged between them.

Active Inventory

The on-floor operational layer — items available for direct use or issuing through a formal request workflow. Special tools, shared equipment and consumables flow through structured request, issue, return and consume workflows. Every action is logged in real time with timestamp, person, quantity and location.

Key Features

Unit-Level Tracking Across All Three Types

Every individual unit of every item carries a status at all times — in reserve, assigned, under maintenance, restricted, missing or damaged. The status updates automatically as items move through operational workflows.

Grouped View with Per-Unit Drill-Down

Reserve presents item groups for efficient scanning of bulk stock, with per-unit detail accessible on expansion — simultaneously usable as a quick stock check and a precise unit-level audit tool.

Flat Unit View in Distributable Reserve

All units in Distributable Reserve are visible without grouping — reflecting the individual-assignment nature of hand tool management and making issuer verification and toolbox management faster.

Inter-Employee Item and Material Transfer

Items and measured quantities can be transferred between two employees directly through Sapphire, with the transfer logged against both parties. Accountability follows the person currently in possession — not the person it was originally issued to.

Real-Time Request and Consume Flows

Active Inventory operates on live request, issue and consume workflows — no batched entries, no end-of-day reconciliation. Every movement is recorded at the moment it happens, producing an accurate, searchable log.

Paperless Audit Trail

Every inventory action — issue, return, transfer, consumption, maintenance dispatch, restriction, recovery — is logged automatically. The records are structured, timestamped and searchable — a queryable operational database that generates analytics without additional effort.

Cross-Inventory Unit Visibility

An item's journey from Reserve to Distributable Reserve to Active Inventory and back is tracked as a continuous record. At any point, a manager can see where every unit is, how it got there and what has happened to it.

Why Do I Need This?

Every operation that uses physical tools, equipment and materials faces the same eventual reality: at some point, items are missing, counts are wrong and no one can explain when it happened or who was responsible. This is not a failure of honesty — it is a failure of structure. When there is no systematic record of who had what and when, the operation runs on memory and assumption until an incident forces a manual count, which is already too late.

The three-inventory structure in Sapphire exists because the solution to this problem has to match the operational reality — not simplify it into a single undifferentiated list. A stored spare part and a personally assigned wrench and a shared calibration instrument are three fundamentally different operational objects. Treating them the same produces records that are technically complete and practically useless.

The inter-employee transfer feature addresses a specific accountability gap that paper-based systems structurally cannot solve. When field staff informally share tools or materials — which happens constantly in real operations — the original assignment record becomes inaccurate the moment the item changes hands. Sapphire makes that informal action a formal one with a 10-second entry, and in doing so, preserves the accountability chain without disrupting the workflow.

The paperless record is not about compliance for its own sake. It is about the difference between an operation that can answer questions — what happened to this item, who had it, when did its condition change, how much of this material was consumed last month — and one that cannot. The analytics, the audit trails and the searchable history that Sapphire generates are a direct product of the small, real-time entries your team makes as part of their normal workflow. No additional effort. No separate reporting step.

[ Request Management — Module Tour ]

What Is Request Management?

Request Management is the movement layer of Sapphire — the module that governs every interaction between a person and an item. It does not matter whether someone is picking up a tool for a job, returning equipment after use, moving an asset to a different location, surrendering an assigned item or drawing a measured quantity of material — every one of these actions follows a defined workflow, passes through the appropriate approval authority and produces a permanent, timestamped record.

The goal is not bureaucracy. The goal is that when something is missing, damaged, misplaced or disputed, the answer already exists in the system — without anyone needing to reconstruct events from memory.

The Five Request Types

Item Issue

An employee raises a request to draw an item from Active Inventory. Routed to the appropriate approver based on role and inventory. On approval, the issuer dispenses the item, inventory status updates and the issue is logged — who requested, who approved, who issued, which item, from which inventory, at what time, from which location. Accountability established at the moment of issue.

Item Return

When an item comes back, its condition is assessed, the handover is recorded and inventory status updates. If damaged, damage is logged at return — not discovered later. The return references the original issue, creating a complete custody chain: issued in this condition, returned in this condition.

Location Change

When an item moves between registered locations — rooms, floors, buildings — the location change formalises that movement with a chain-of-custody record. Origin, destination, approving authority and timestamp — every movement event is permanently logged.

Surrender

De-assignment of permanently assigned items — tools from a toolbox, equipment from a workstation. Raised with a reason code, condition recorded at collection, item returned to the appropriate inventory. Links to the original assignment, closing the custody loop.

Issue Blends

Composite materials — lubricant blends, chemical solutions, paint mixes — drawn from multiple parent materials with precise quantities, batch references and parent-child linkage. Every contributing component is traceable. Inventory levels update for every contributing material at point of issue.

Key Features

Five Structurally Distinct Request Types

Each request type reflects a different operational event — not a variation of the same action. Issue, return, location change, surrender and blend issue each have their own workflow, their own approval logic and their own record structure.

Role-Based Approval Routing

Requests are routed to the correct approving authority automatically based on the requester's role and the inventory involved. A request raised by a general employee does not land on the wrong person's screen — it reaches the designated issuer or manager.

Real-Time Inventory Status Updates

Every approved and completed request updates inventory status immediately. There is no batch reconciliation, no end-of-day sync and no manual update step. The moment an item is issued, returned, moved or surrendered, its record reflects the new state.

Complete Audit Trail Per Request

Every request carries a permanent record of every action taken on it — raised, reviewed, approved or rejected, issued, confirmed received. Each action is timestamped and attributed to the person who took it. The trail cannot be edited or deleted.

Volumetric Issue and Return Precision

Materials dispensed or returned in measured quantities are recorded with the exact volume, weight or unit count involved. Partial returns are handled accurately. Blend components are tracked individually. The inventory mathematics are correct at every point in the workflow.

Condition Recording at Issue and Return

Item condition is captured at both ends of the custody period — when issued and when returned. This makes condition comparison objective, documented and unambiguous. Disputes about when damage occurred are resolved by the record, not by recollection.

Why Do I Need This?

The default state of most operations is informal. Tools are picked up, used and returned without ceremony because that is the fastest way to get work done. This works fine until something goes missing, something comes back damaged or someone disputes what was issued and what was returned. At that point, the operation has no record to consult — only people's recollections, which differ.

Request Management does not slow down the workflow. A request raised in Sapphire takes seconds. An approval takes one action. The issue takes one confirmation. The entire cycle — request to issue — takes less time than filling out a paper register entry and produces a record that is infinitely more useful.

The more significant value is operational visibility. When every request is in the system, management can see — in real time — what is currently issued, what is pending approval, what has not been returned and what has come back in poor condition. This is not a report generated at the end of the month. It is the live operational picture of every item interaction happening across the facility right now.

For materials specifically — fluids, chemicals, consumables — the blend issue and volumetric tracking features transform what is typically the most poorly recorded category of inventory into a precise, auditable consumption record. The data this produces is directly useful for procurement planning, cost allocation and wastage analysis.

[ Inspection & Reporting — Module Tour ]

What Is Inspection & Reporting?

Inspection in Sapphire is not a checklist that someone fills in and forgets. It is a structured assessment cycle built on objective criteria — where every checkpoint has a defined evaluation standard, every result is scored against that standard and every completed inspection becomes a permanent, comparable data point in the asset's condition history.

Reporting is the layer that makes this continuous record actionable — aggregating inspection results across assets, locations, inspectors and time periods into visible, analysable data.

How It Works

Step 1
Define Inspection Checkpoints

Checkpoints are defined with condition categories and scoring rules or damage criteria. This standardisation is what makes the inspection objective. The question is never "does this look okay to you?" It is always "does this meet the defined condition standard?"

Step 2
Set the Inspection Schedule

Inspection cycles are configured as daily, weekly, monthly or custom intervals. Sapphire generates inspection tasks automatically against the schedule. The scheduling engine ensures non-overlapping reports — one record per period, no duplicates, no gaps.

Step 3
Receive and Action Smart Reminders

When an inspection task is due, the assigned inspector receives a timed alert. If the inspection is not completed within the defined window, escalation reminders follow. The schedule does not rely on someone remembering.

Step 4
Conduct the Inspection

The inspector evaluates the asset against each checkpoint in sequence. For each checkpoint, the condition is recorded against the predefined criteria. Photos, notes and supporting documents can be attached. Digital signatures are collected at completion where required.

Step 5
Generate the Inspection Record

On completion, Sapphire generates a structured inspection record — asset identity, inspector identity, timestamp, checkpoint-by-checkpoint findings, overall condition score and attached evidence. Permanent, searchable and exportable.

Step 6
Review Reports and Track Trends

Inspection results accumulate into the reporting layer, becoming analysable across multiple dimensions. Recurring findings or declining condition scores over successive inspections surface patterns automatically.

Key Features

Objective Checkpoint Architecture

Every inspection is evaluated against predefined condition categories and scoring rules — not against individual judgment. Two inspectors using the same checkpoint criteria produce directly comparable results. Subjectivity is structurally eliminated.

Non-Overlapping Inspection Scheduling

The scheduling engine tracks completed inspections against each cycle window and generates new tasks only when the previous window has been covered. Inspection history is clean — one record per period, no duplicates, no gaps.

Automated Task Generation and Reminders

Inspection tasks are generated automatically against the configured schedule. Timed reminders push to the assigned inspector. Escalations follow if the window is missed. The inspection programme runs without manual coordination.

Digital Evidence Capture

Photos, notes, documents and digital signatures are captured within the inspection workflow at the checkpoint level. Supporting evidence is attached to the specific finding it documents — not filed separately where context is lost.

Paperless Condition Records

Every completed inspection is a structured digital record immediately — no transcription, no filing, no risk of loss. The record is searchable by any field and exportable for compliance submissions, regulatory audits or internal reviews.

Trend Reporting Across Any Dimension

Inspection results are reportable by asset, location, asset type, inspector, condition category or time period in any combination. Trend lines across successive inspection cycles make deterioration visible before it becomes failure.

Complete Audit Trail

Every inspection record carries the inspector's identity, the timestamp and a change history. No inspection result can be altered without a corresponding audit entry. Compliance submissions are supported by records that are independently verifiable.

Why Do I Need This?

The honest answer to why inspections are poorly done in most operations is that the standard inspection process produces effort without proportionate return. Filling in a paper form takes time. The form gets filed. Nobody looks at it again unless something goes wrong — at which point the relevant forms cannot be found, or when found, contain entries so inconsistent across inspectors that they prove nothing.

Sapphire's inspection module changes the return on that effort. The time invested in conducting a structured digital inspection using objective checkpoints produces a permanent, comparable record that immediately joins an asset's condition history. That history generates trend data. That trend data surfaces deterioration before it becomes breakdown. The inspection becomes an input into a predictive picture of the asset's condition — not a compliance checkbox that disappears into a filing cabinet.

The objective checkpoint structure specifically addresses the reliability problem. When inspection criteria are defined and standardised, the findings from an inspection conducted three months ago by one inspector are directly comparable to findings from today's inspection conducted by a different inspector. This comparability is what makes trend analysis meaningful and what makes inspection records credible as compliance evidence.

The non-overlapping schedule feature exists because inspection history should be clean. An asset should have one inspection record per cycle period — not three records from the same week because tasks were generated without checking completion status, and not a gap of six weeks because nobody noticed the schedule had been missed.

What Is Reporting & Resolution?

Reporting & Resolution is the lifecycle accountability module of Sapphire — the system that handles what happens after an asset or material is flagged as damaged, missing, expired, mishandled or unfit. It provides structured resolution workflows for three asset outcomes — repair, replacement and discard — and dedicated material management workflows for expiry, mishandling and contamination. Every resolution is documented with costs, providers, evidence and audit trails.

The full ownership cost of every asset decision is captured as it happens, not reconstructed later.

Asset Resolution Workflows

Repair

Complete service record — provider, repair work, parts used, costs, timeline, warranty status. Linked to the original report. Successive repairs accumulate into a repair history informing the next repair-versus-replace decision with actual cost data.

Replacement

Procurement details of the new asset, linkage between old and new records, disposal of the old asset and depreciation adjustments. The successor inherits the operational context while the predecessor's record is formally closed with total accumulated cost.

Discard

Formally closes the asset record — disposal reason, method, salvage value captured and final lifecycle cost calculated. The record is preserved as a permanent historical entry contributing to lifecycle analytics and future procurement decisions.

Material Resolution Workflows

Expired Materials

Expiry tracked by batch, lot or volume. Expired material flagged for discard or vendor exchange — with volumes in/out, costs, new expiry dates and multi-lot partitioning at receipt. Expired material can continue in use with the flag recorded in the consumption log.

Mishandled Materials

Spillage, container residue, handling loss — formally recorded with cause and quantity. Inventory remains accurate. Loss patterns become visible in reporting, identifying whether mishandling losses indicate a process problem worth addressing.

Contaminated & Unfit Materials

Materials rendered unfit through contamination, chemical reaction, improper storage or environmental events — power failures, accidental mixing, heat degradation — recorded with cause, volume affected and unfitness determination. Documentation supports vendor exchange or insurance claims.

Key Features

Three Structured Resolution Outcomes

Repair, replace and discard each have a dedicated workflow with specific fields for the information each outcome requires. No single generic "resolve" action that loses the distinction between fixing something and discarding it.

Full Cost Integration Per Resolution

Every resolution captures its associated costs at the line-item level — parts, labour, service provider, procurement cost, salvage value. The costs attach to the resolution record and aggregate into the asset's total cost of ownership.

Invoice and Document Attachment

Bills, service invoices, vendor receipts, disposal certificates and any supporting documentation are attached directly to the resolution record they relate to. Evidence lives with the record — not in a separate filing system.

Linked Audit Trail from Report to Resolution

Every resolution record references the report that initiated it. The chain from "item flagged" to "item resolved" is permanently traceable — who reported it, what resolution was chosen, who carried it out, what it cost and when it was completed.

Vendor Exchange Workflow with Batch Partitioning

Expired material exchanges are documented completely — volumes in, volumes out, costs, new expiry dates. Multi-lot incoming batches are partitioned at receipt with individual expiry dates per partition. Inventory reflects the new material accurately from the moment of exchange.

Asset Lifecycle Cost Calculation

At discard, Sapphire calculates the complete lifetime cost — purchase, all repairs, all replacement parts, maintenance costs — producing the true cost of ownership figure that procurement and replacement decisions should be based on.

Why Do I Need This?

Every operation makes repair, replace and discard decisions constantly. The problem is that in most operations, these decisions are made without access to the accumulated cost history of the asset in question. A machine that has been repaired four times in eighteen months, with each repair costing between Rs. 8,000 and Rs. 25,000, should have been replaced after the second repair. But without a record of all four repairs in one place, each repair decision looks isolated — and isolation makes each one seem reasonable.

Sapphire's resolution module exists to end the isolation of individual decisions. Every repair record, every replacement record, every discard record is linked to the asset it relates to and to every other resolution record for that same asset. The accumulated picture is always visible. The total cost of ownership is always current.

The material management features address a category of operational loss that most asset management systems do not handle at all. Expired materials, mishandling losses and contamination events happen in every operation that uses physical materials — but they are almost never formally recorded, which means they are never accounted for, never analysed and never addressed. Sapphire records them with the same rigour applied to every other operational event.

[ Service & Maintenance — Module Tour ]

What Is Service & Maintenance?

Service & Maintenance is Sapphire's preventive maintenance engine — the module that ensures every asset in the operation is serviced on schedule, every service event is completely documented and every cost associated with maintaining an asset is captured and attributed correctly. It operates at the individual unit level, recognises the operational reality that identical equipment can have significantly different maintenance needs depending on workload, and accommodates the full range of real-world scheduling disruptions without losing cycle integrity or producing inaccurate records.

How It Works

Step 1
Configure Service Cycles Per Unit

Service schedules are defined at the individual unit level — not at the item type level. Two lifts of the same model, one running 100 cycles a day and one running 20, do not share a maintenance schedule. Each unit carries its own configured cycle — daily, weekly, monthly, or custom — set independently based on actual operational demand.

Step 2
Receive Automated Reminders

When a service window approaches, Sapphire generates a reminder for the responsible party — early enough to arrange the service provider, order required spare parts and schedule the downtime. Not on the day the service is already overdue.

Step 3
Handle Scheduling Disruptions Cleanly

When a scheduled service cannot be performed, the cycle day is marked as skipped with a recorded reason. The cycle continues from the original schedule — not from the skip date. A skipped entry does not corrupt the history or shift the cycle incorrectly.

Step 4
Reset the Cycle When Needed

When a major service event occurs significantly ahead of schedule — emergency repair, pre-emptive full service — the cycle is reset from that date with an optional period adjustment. Local to that unit only. Other units of the same type are unaffected.

Step 5
Log the Service Event

Every completed service event is logged with complete documentation — provider, representative, labour hours, spare parts with part numbers and costs, invoice attachments, photos and notes. Linked to the specific service cycle it fulfils.

Step 6
Link Coverage and Benefits

Each asset's service record is connected to its coverage context — active service contracts, insurance policies, warranties and extended coverage plans. Expiry dates, coverage limits and renewal alerts prevent lapsed coverage. Claims are recorded against the events that generated them.

Key Features

Per-Unit Service Cycle Configuration

Every individual unit carries its own independently configured service schedule. Same model, different workload — different cycle. Changes to one unit's schedule do not affect any other unit.

Skip With Reason — Cycle Integrity Preserved

Missed or early service events are recorded as skips with documented reasons. The underlying cycle continues from its original schedule without distortion. The history shows accurately what happened and why.

Unscheduled Service Cycle Reset

When a major early service event resets the practical maintenance baseline, the cycle is reset from that date with an optional period adjustment — local to that unit only. The next reminder is always accurate to operational reality.

Complete Service Event Documentation

Provider, representative, labour hours, spare parts with part numbers and costs, invoice attachments, photos and notes — every service event is logged with the full detail needed to reconstruct exactly what was done, by whom, at what cost and with what materials.

Coverage and Benefits Visibility

Service contracts, insurance policies, warranties and extended coverage are linked to assets and visible in their service context. Expiry and renewal alerts prevent lapsed coverage. Claims are recorded against the events that generated them.

Spare Parts History Per Asset

Every spare part used in every service event is recorded against the asset that received it — part number, vendor, quantity, cost. The accumulated spare parts history supports predictive maintenance planning and procurement forecasting.

Full Financial Integration

All service costs roll up to total cost of ownership dashboards — attributable by asset, location, vendor and period. Maintenance budget forecasting is supported by actual historical cost data rather than estimates.

Why Do I Need This?

The most expensive maintenance event in any operation is the unplanned one. An unplanned breakdown interrupts production, grounds a vehicle, takes a critical piece of equipment offline and typically costs several times what a scheduled service would have cost — because it involves emergency callout rates, expedited parts procurement, unplanned downtime and the downstream costs of whatever the equipment failure disrupted.

The per-unit cycle configuration addresses the most common reason preventive maintenance schedules become inaccurate over time: identical equipment does not age identically. A pump running continuously in a high-temperature environment degrades faster than the same pump model running intermittently at ambient temperature. Applying the manufacturer's standard service interval equally to both will service one correctly and the other incorrectly.

The skip and reset features exist because the alternative — forcing every service event to fit a rigid schedule that does not accommodate real-world disruption — produces one of two outcomes: either the records are falsified to show compliance that did not happen, or the schedule is abandoned because it cannot be maintained in practice. Sapphire's approach produces an honest record of what actually happened, preserves cycle integrity and ensures the next reminder is always calibrated to operational reality.

What Is Smart Analytics?

Smart Analytics is Sapphire's intelligence layer — the module that aggregates every data point generated across all other modules and presents it as actionable, real-time operational insight. It does not require separate data entry, manual report compilation or periodic exports. Every transaction recorded anywhere in Sapphire flows automatically into the analytics framework and becomes immediately visible in the relevant dashboard.

The result is an operational picture that is always current, always complete and always structured around the decisions it needs to support.

What It Covers

Cost Analysis & Asset Lifecycle

Capital Allocation Heatmap, Price Benchmarking Grid, Procurement Trend Timeline and Depreciation Intelligence — tracking every financial event under Companies Act 2013 and IT Act 1961 methods.

Maintenance & Protection Costs

Consolidated Protection Overview, Protection Individual Items and Protection Category Grid — bringing warranty, insurance and maintenance contract spending into aggregate visibility.

Inspection & Reporting Analytics

Real-time Inspection Tracking, Report Submission Velocity and Inspection vs Report Correlation — revealing whether proactive inspections are preventing failures or reactive reporting is dominating.

OPEX Management

OPEX Overview, Net Expense After Recoveries, OPEX Trend Timeline and OPEX Asset Monitoring — separating labour, parts and overhead cost trends to make repair-versus-replace decisions evidence-based.

Disposal & Recovery

Disposal Waterfall Analysis, Category Resale Value Retention and Disposal Timeline — tracking value erosion from purchase to salvage, informing procurement based on actual resale performance.

Material Intelligence

Employee Consumption, Equipment Allocation and Waste & Damage Accounting — linking material usage to individuals and assets, separating productive consumption from operational loss.

Request Analytics

Demand Frequency, Return Condition Analysis and Floor Time vs Shelf Time — revealing which equipment drives genuine demand, identifying chronic damage patterns and distinguishing high-utilisation from idle inventory.

Material Request Analytics

Complete Consumption Analysis and Peak Demand Timing — distinguishing fully consumed materials from partial returns, identifying when materials are most frequently requested for strategic inventory positioning.

Blend Consumption Tracking

Issue frequency, requester patterns, timeframe demand and volume optimisation for blended materials — supporting preparation scheduling, batch sizing decisions and waste reduction from expired quantities.

Vehicle Mileage Tracking, Fuel Economy Analysis and Economy Performance Feed — recording every assignment and mileage update, comparing efficiency across vehicles, flagging outliers whose consumption indicates maintenance requirements.

Key Features

Real-Time Across All Dimensions

Every dashboard reflects the current state of the operation — updated as transactions occur, not as reports are generated. No reporting cycle, no data export step, no lag between operational reality and analytical visibility.

Depreciation Under Indian Statutory Methods

Companies Act 2013 written down value and IT Act 1961 block depreciation are both supported — producing compliance-ready schedules without manual calculation or external tools.

Net Cost After Recoveries

OPEX figures are presented net of insurance, warranty and contract recoveries — reflecting actual out-of-pocket expenditure rather than gross repair spend, the number that matters for operational budget management.

Total Cost of Ownership Per Asset

Purchase cost, all maintenance costs, all repair costs, all parts costs and final salvage value are accumulated per asset throughout its lifecycle — producing a true ownership cost figure that makes replacement decisions evidence-based.

Infinite Filtering on All Registers

Every register in Sapphire — requests, reports, inspections, resolutions, consumption, transfers — supports filtering across any combination of dimensions. Any slice of the operational record is accessible without custom report configuration.

Tamper-Proof Audit Trail

The universal activity log and all workflow registers are immutable — every entry is permanent and attributed. Compliance verification, dispute resolution and regulatory audit support are available instantly, not after weeks of document recovery.

Why Do I Need This?

The operational data already exists in every facility — it is generated by every transaction that happens every day. The problem is that without a system capturing it in structured form at the moment it occurs, it exists only as paper records, memory and informal knowledge. None of these are queryable. None produce trends. None support decisions at the level of precision that the data, if it existed in structured form, would enable.

Sapphire's analytics layer does not require additional effort to produce its outputs. The dashboards, the cost analyses, the trend timelines and the compliance registers are all direct products of the operational entries made across every other module — requests raised, inspections completed, services logged, materials consumed. The analytical value is a byproduct of accurate operational recording.

The compliance dimension is frequently underestimated until it is urgently needed. When an auditor requires documentation of every maintenance event for a regulated asset class, or an insurance claim requires proof of regular inspection, or a dispute requires the custody history of a specific item — the answer is either immediately available in Sapphire's registers or it is not available at all. The tamper-proof audit trail and the workflow-specific registers are operational insurance — the cost of maintaining them is zero, because they are maintained automatically.
